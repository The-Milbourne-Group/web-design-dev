# SOP: SCOPE CHANGE

**System:** Delivery
**Purpose:** Evaluate and govern any material change to an engagement's objectives, deliverables, integrations, or assumptions.
**Authority:** Tier 4 procedure.
**Parent Authority:** `MASTER.md` (Tier 1 — supreme), `DELIVERY.md` §5, `SERVICES.md` §5
**Applies to:** Every mid-engagement change request, from any source.
**Owner:** Founder
**Status:** Active
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-29
**Review trigger:** Recurring scope disputes; margin erosion.
**Related:** `DELIVERY.md`, `SERVICES.md`, `SALES.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

`DELIVERY.md` §5 and `SERVICES.md` §5 both require material changes to be
evaluated before implementation, but no procedure existed to do it. Scope
creep is the primary margin risk in fixed-scope engagements: it arrives as a
sequence of individually reasonable requests, each too small to refuse.

## 2. Trigger

Any request or discovery that changes objectives, deliverables, integrations,
requirements, timeline, or a stated assumption — whether raised by the client,
the founder, or found during implementation.

## 3. Owner

Founder. Only the founder may accept, decline, or reprice a scope change.

## 4. Inputs

- The request, in the requester's own words
- Current project brief, agreement, and acceptance criteria
- Work completed and remaining
- Current timeline and capacity

## 5. Procedure

1. **Capture the request in writing** before evaluating it. An unrecorded
   verbal request is the origin of most scope disputes.

2. **Classify it:**

   | Class | Definition | Handling |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Clarification** | Refines something already in scope | Proceed; record it |
   | **Minor** | Small addition, no timeline or cost impact | Founder decides; record |
   | **Material** | Changes deliverables, integrations, timeline, or cost | Change order required |
   | **Directional** | Changes the engagement's objective | Stop; re-scope the engagement |

   When uncertain, classify one level higher.

3. **Assess impact** on scope, effort, timeline, cost, dependencies, risk,
   quality, and already-completed work. State whether prior work is
   invalidated.

4. **Check strategic fit.** Does the change keep the engagement within a stage
   of the commercial progression (`SERVICES.md` §2)? A change that turns an
   entry engagement into an expansion project is a new engagement, not a
   change order.

5. **Decide** — accept, accept with change order, defer to a later phase, or
   decline. Recommending deferral to a future engagement is often the correct
   answer and preserves the relationship.

6. **Document a Material or Directional change as a change order** stating
   what changes, the revised deliverables, the timeline impact, the commercial
   impact, and what remains unchanged. Obtain written client approval before
   implementing.

7. **Update the project brief** so it continues to describe the actual
   engagement.

8. **Log it** in the client decision log. Where a change reveals a defect in a
   company document, raise a proposal under
   `governance/CHANGE_MANAGEMENT.md` §4 — do not edit company documents from
   inside a project.

## 6. Outputs

- Written record of the request and its classification
- Impact assessment
- Change order where Material or Directional, with client approval
- Updated project brief
- Entry in the client decision log

## 7. Quality Checks

- [ ] Request is recorded in writing before evaluation.
- [ ] Classification is justified, not assumed.
- [ ] Timeline and commercial impact are stated even when zero.
- [ ] No Material change was implemented before written approval.
- [ ] The cumulative effect of accumulated Minor changes has been reviewed.
- [ ] The project brief reflects the current engagement.

## 8. Failure Conditions & Recovery

| Failure | Recovery |
|---|---|
| Change implemented before approval | Stop; document; escalate; the company now carries unagreed work |
| Many Minor changes have eroded margin | Review cumulatively; a pattern of small changes is one Material change |
| Client asserts the change was always in scope | Return to the agreement and brief; resolve in writing, not from memory |
| Change invalidates completed work | State this explicitly in the impact assessment before the client decides |
| Founder accepted a change to preserve goodwill | Record it as a commercial decision with its cost, so the pattern is visible |

## 9. Escalation

Every Material and Directional change is by definition a founder decision. Also
escalate when: the client disputes the classification; the change would make
the engagement unprofitable; or the request cannot be delivered responsibly.

## 10. Automation Potential

**Can be assisted:** capturing and classifying requests, drafting impact
assessments and change orders, tracking cumulative Minor changes.

**Must not be automated:** accepting, declining, or repricing any change, and
any commercial communication to the client.
