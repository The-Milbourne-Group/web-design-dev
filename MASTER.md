# 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

## 1.1 Purpose

This document defines **how the company operates**: the universal principles,
execution protocol, quality standards, and authority boundaries that apply
across every domain.

It is deliberately not a container for business knowledge. Detailed knowledge
belongs in the domain source-of-truth documents listed in §14, and this
document must never duplicate them. When this document and a domain document
describe the same subject, one of them is wrong.

**What governs what:**

| Question | Document |
|---|---|
| **How do we operate, verify, and decide?** | **This document — supreme** |
| Which document wins in a conflict? | `governance/AUTHORITY.md`, under §5 of this document |
| What is the business, and what has the founder decided? | `STRATEGIC_CONFIGURATION.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, under §2 of this document |
| What is true about a specific domain? | The Tier 3 owner listed in §14 |

## 1.2 Scope

Unless a more specific authorized rule applies, this document governs
business strategy, marketing, sales, client delivery, design, development,
automation, AI agents, internal operations, documentation, quality assurance,
project execution, client data handling, and technical decision-making.

## 1.3 What This Document Is Not

Not a client brief, technical specification, service catalog, collection of
temporary notes, repository coding guide, replacement for SOPs, replacement
for client documentation, or a place to store credentials.

Do not turn this document into an uncontrolled collection of unrelated
instructions. Content that violates this section must be relocated to its
proper domain, not tolerated.

## 1.4 Operating Principle

The objective is not maximum activity. The objective is not maximum AI usage.

> Produce measurable client and business value through reliable, profitable,
> scalable systems with the minimum necessary manual effort.

# 2. COMPANY IDENTITY AND MISSION

## 2.1 Identity

**Company Name:** The Milbourne Group

**Commercial identity:** A boutique strategic digital systems partner helping
established businesses strengthen the digital infrastructure that supports
credibility, conversion, operational efficiency, and growth.

**Primary problem addressed:** Fragmented, outgrown, or underperforming
digital infrastructure.

**Operating model:** Founder-led strategy, sales, solution architecture, and
quality control, supported by documented systems, automation, AI leverage, and
flexible specialist capacity.

**Focus model:** A narrow problem presented externally; broad capability
retained internally.

## 2.2 The Role of AI and Automation

AI and automation are **operating capabilities**, not the company's identity
or its market-facing value proposition.

They increase delivery capacity, improve consistency, and reduce unnecessary
manual work. They are never the reason a client should buy.

The company does not position itself as an AI-first or AI-native business,
and does not compete as a generic AI agency. Differentiation comes from
business understanding, strategic thinking, implementation quality, systems
expertise, appropriate human oversight, and measurable business improvement.

*Authority: D-001, D-002, D-007, D-009 in `DECISIONS.md`. Any document, agent,
or artifact contradicting this section is defective and must be corrected.*

## 2.3 Mission

Help established businesses improve how they present themselves, attract
demand, convert opportunities, and operate — through better digital systems.

## 2.4 Long-Term Vision

A highly systemized digital services company with increasing reliability,
efficiency, profitability, and capacity: documented systems, specialized
capabilities, automation, reusable assets, and appropriate human oversight.

# 3. BUSINESS OBJECTIVES AND DECISION PRIORITY

## 3.1 Primary Objective

Build a profitable, reliable, scalable business that produces measurable
client outcomes.

## 3.2 Decision Priority

When priorities compete, resolve in this order:

1. Legal, contractual, security, and safety obligations
2. Client commitments already made
3. Revenue and margin quality
4. Delivery reliability
5. System leverage and reusability
6. Capability expansion

## 3.3 Outcome Standard

Work is measured by the outcome it produces, not by the volume of artifacts
generated. A deliverable that does not change a client or business result has
not created value.

# 4. CORE OPERATING PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Outcomes Over Activity
Measure results, not effort. Do not confuse motion with progress.

## 4.2 Simplicity Before Complexity
Choose the simplest approach that solves the problem reliably. Complexity must
be justified by a requirement, not by preference or capability.

## 4.3 Systems Before Repetition
Work performed three times should be documented. Work documented and stable
should be considered for automation under `AUTOMATION.md`.

## 4.4 Verify Before Trusting
AI output, third-party claims, inherited configuration, and prior
documentation are all evidence — not verified truth. Verification effort must
be proportional to consequence.

## 4.5 Protect the Client
Client interests, data, and confidentiality outrank convenience, speed, and
internal preference.

## 4.6 Improve the System, Not Only the Output
When work reveals a weakness in a process, document, or automation, fix the
system — not just the current instance.

## 4.7 Never Fabricate
Do not invent facts, metrics, capabilities, credentials, client results, or
decisions the founder has not made. When a value is unknown, say so and
consult `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`. This principle has no exceptions and no deadline
override.

# 5. GOVERNANCE AND SOURCE OF TRUTH

## 5.1 Authority

**This document is the ultimate authority.** Nothing in this repository
outranks `MASTER.md`.

The detailed hierarchy, scope rules, decision rights, and conflict-resolution
procedure are set out in **`governance/AUTHORITY.md`**, which holds that role
by delegation from this section and is subordinate to this document.

```
Tier 1  MASTER.md — this document, supreme
Tier 2  Founder-approved strategy (STRATEGIC_CONFIGURATION.md, DECISIONS.md)
Tier 3  Domain sources of truth (§14) and the registers
Tier 4  SOPs
Tier 5  Agent instructions
Tier 6  Templates
Tier 7  Project and client knowledge
Tier 8  Temporary working artifacts
```

**The absolute constraints of §7 bind every tier, including this one.** Law,
executed contracts, security, safety, and client confidentiality are
non-negotiable because *this document* makes them so — not because anything
ranks above it. `MASTER.md` can therefore never be cited to justify
overriding them; the rule being invoked against is its own.

**Delegation.** This document is deliberately thin. It governs conduct and
delegates domain substance to Tier 3 and strategic identity to Tier 2 (§2).
A delegated document binds because this one says it does; where a delegated
document contradicts this one, this one wins and the other is defective.

Authority is **scoped** as well as tiered: a document governs only its
declared subject. Within a subject, the most specific document at the highest
applicable tier wins.

*This replaces the instruction hierarchy previously stated in this document,
which ranked `MASTER.md` below domain documents (Level 5 beneath Level 4)
while simultaneously claiming in §1.1 to be the root constitution. See
`DECISIONS.md` D-012.*

## 5.2 Conflict Resolution

1. Identify the conflicting instructions and their sources.
2. Apply the absolute constraints of §7 if implicated — they settle it.
3. Discard any document acting outside its scope.
4. Apply tier, then specificity.
5. If unresolved, record in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, take the conservative
   interpretation, and escalate.
6. Correct the defective document.

Never silently ignore a material contradiction. A conflict resolved in
conversation but left on disk will recur.

## 5.3 Source-of-Truth Rule

Every important category of information has exactly one authoritative
location, recorded in `governance/DOCUMENT_REGISTRY.md`.

Never maintain competing versions of client requirements, pricing, technical
architecture, business strategy, brand standards, or processes. If duplication
is unavoidable, the copy must name its authoritative source and must not be
edited independently.

## 5.4 Context Discipline

More context is not better context. Load the smallest set of documents
necessary to complete the work reliably — see `CLAUDE.md` for the loading
protocol.

Avoid irrelevant context, duplicate instructions, outdated requirements,
conflicting copies, and — as an absolute constraint under §7.1 — context from
unrelated clients.

# 6. UNIVERSAL EXECUTION PROTOCOL

For significant work, follow this sequence. Depth must match consequence;
a small reversible task does not require a formal plan.

## 6.1 Understand
What is requested? What outcome is desired? Who is affected? What problem is
actually being solved? What constraints apply?

## 6.2 Gather Context
Consult the relevant sources of truth. Do not begin complex work on
assumptions when authoritative information exists.

## 6.3 Assess Information Sufficiency
Classify what is missing:

- **Critical** — cannot proceed reliably. Escalate.
- **Important** — proceed with an assumption recorded in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`.
- **Optional** — proceed.

Do not interrupt productive work for optional information. Do not proceed past
critical gaps by inventing values.

## 6.4 Plan
For substantial work determine objective, approach, dependencies, constraints,
risks, alternatives, sequence, and verification method.

## 6.5 Execute
Prefer existing patterns, reusable assets, clear structures, modular
solutions, and small verifiable changes.

## 6.6 Verify
Confirm the success criteria are met. Verification may include requirement
review, functional testing, responsive testing, accessibility review,
performance checks, security review, integration testing, and regression
testing — matched to the nature of the work.

## 6.7 Document
Update documentation when work changes architecture, processes, important
decisions, requirements, automation behavior, or known limitations. Route
changes through `governance/CHANGE_MANAGEMENT.md`. Do not create
documentation with no future operational value.

## 6.8 Improve
Afterward: What caused unnecessary effort? What could fail next time? What can
be reused, documented, or automated? What should remain manual?

# 7. CLIENT PROTECTION AND SECURITY

*These are the **absolute constraints**. Nothing in this section is negotiable
by any document, client request, agent instruction, deadline, or founder
convenience — and no other part of this document may be read to permit an
exception. Operational detail is in `SECURITY.md`.*

## 7.1 Client Context Isolation

Every client has isolated context. Never mix credentials, private strategy,
customer information, analytics, proprietary documents, or internal systems
between unrelated clients.

Load exactly one client's context per working session. If work requires two
clients, stop and escalate.

## 7.2 Minimum Necessary Access

Use only the information and permissions necessary for the assigned task. Do
not request or retain unnecessary access.

## 7.3 Sensitive Information

Never place credentials or secrets in public repositories, public
documentation, client-facing content, unjustified logs, or unsecured
configuration. Document secrets by **name and location only** — never by
value.

## 7.4 Authorization Boundaries

Do not independently perform consequential actions outside authorized
authority. These always require explicit approval:

- Financial transactions and contractual commitments
- Destructive or irreversible production actions
- Credential creation, rotation, or disclosure
- Sensitive data disclosure
- Irreversible infrastructure changes
- Client-facing communication that commits the company
- Publishing anything externally visible

When authority is unclear, it is absent. Escalate.

# 8. INFORMATION, ASSUMPTIONS, AND UNCERTAINTY

## 8.1 Missing Information

Do not invent information. Identify what is missing, determine its importance,
continue where a reasonable path exists, and request clarification only when
necessary.

Before supplying any strategic, commercial, or client-profile value, check
`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`. If it is registered there, it is unknown — and must not
be produced.

## 8.2 Assumptions

When an assumption materially affects the outcome: state it, record it in
`ASSUMPTIONS.md`, label it in the output, and proceed unless clarification is
critical. Never present an assumption as an established fact.

## 8.3 Uncertainty

Identify the uncertainty, explain the risk, identify what would reduce it, and
recommend the best available action. Do not fabricate certainty.

# 9. QUALITY AND COMPLETION STANDARDS

## 9.1 Universal Quality Standard

Evaluate meaningful work for correctness, completeness, relevance, clarity,
maintainability, reliability, security, efficiency, and business value.

## 9.2 Definition of Done

A task is complete only when:

- The relevant requirements are satisfied
- The deliverable actually exists
- Appropriate verification has occurred
- Material limitations are disclosed
- Required documentation is updated

A generated output is not automatically a completed outcome.

## 9.3 Verification Honesty

Never claim that testing, deployment, delivery, or verification occurred when
it did not. State the actual verification status, including what was **not**
checked.

This is an absolute rule. A false completion claim is more damaging than an
incomplete deliverable, because it removes the opportunity to correct it.

# 10. AI AGENT GOVERNANCE

## 10.1 Agent Principle

Agents are specialized operating roles. They are not assumed to be
independently correct. Agent output is work product, analysis, evidence,
recommendation, or draft — never automatically truth.

Specialization grants **capability**, not **authority**.

## 10.2 Required Agent Definition

Every agent definition must specify all of the following. A definition missing
any element is defective and must not be relied upon:

1. Mission
2. Responsibilities
3. Explicit non-responsibilities
4. Inputs and required context
5. Outputs
6. Tools
7. Decision authority
8. Escalation rules
9. Quality standards
10. Success criteria

The agent registry at `agents/README.md` records which agent handles which
task, what it may decide, and when it must escalate.

## 10.3 Agent Handoffs

Every meaningful handoff states: objective, context, inputs, required output,
constraints, acceptance criteria, and known risks. Avoid vague handoffs.

## 10.4 Universal Agent Constraints

No agent may:

- Decide anything reserved to the founder in `governance/AUTHORITY.md` §6
- Supply a value registered as `Open` in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`
- Contradict `STRATEGIC_CONFIGURATION.md`
- Edit `MASTER.md` or any Tier 2–3 document without going through
  `governance/CHANGE_MANAGEMENT.md`
- Claim verification that did not occur
- Move client context between engagements

# 11. AUTOMATION AUTONOMY

Automation policy, autonomy levels, and the workflow standard are defined in
`AUTOMATION.md`.

Two constitutional constraints apply regardless:

1. **Never automate an undefined process.** A process without a documented
   trigger, inputs, outputs, failure conditions, recovery, and owner is not
   eligible for automation.
2. **Autonomy above human-approved (Level 2) requires founder authorization**
   and monitoring proportional to consequence. Prefer visible, recoverable
   failure over silent incorrect success.

# 12. PRIORITIZATION AND STRATEGIC FOCUS

## 12.1 Priority Evaluation

Evaluate significant opportunities on impact, urgency, effort, risk,
reusability, and automation leverage.

## 12.2 Current Priority

Maintain one clearly defined primary objective. When a new opportunity
appears, ask whether it creates more meaningful value than the work currently
prioritized. If not, defer it unless urgency or risk requires otherwise.

Avoid constant priority switching. Current sequencing is in `ROADMAP.md`.

## 12.3 Bottleneck Analysis

Identify the primary constraint — leads, offer, conversion, delivery speed,
capacity, margin, technical limits, documentation, or automation reliability.

Do not optimize secondary systems while a dominant bottleneck remains.

## 12.4 Highest-Leverage Action

> What action produces the greatest meaningful improvement relative to its
> cost, risk, and effort?

Then decide whether it should be executed, delegated, automated, scheduled,
documented, or deferred.

## 13.1 Repository Context

`templates/development/REPOSITORY_CLAUDE_TEMPLATE.md`.

## 13.2 Repository Authority

Repository instructions govern implementation detail for that repository only.
They never override security requirements, client confidentiality, legal or
contractual requirements, or any absolute constraint under §7.

## 13.3 Project Context

Project-specific information belongs with the project, under `clients/`. Do
not contaminate company documentation with implementation detail, and do not
edit company documents from inside a project — raise a change proposal
instead (`governance/CHANGE_MANAGEMENT.md`).

# 16. FINAL OPERATING RULE

For every significant action, decision, recommendation, process, automation,
or system change, ask:

> Does this materially improve client outcomes, or strengthen The Milbourne
> Group's ability to produce profitable, reliable, scalable results?

If the answer is unclear, reassess the work.

The company moves continuously toward better client outcomes, better business
outcomes, less unnecessary work, more reliable systems, more reusable assets,
better verification, more intelligent automation, greater profitability,
greater scalability, and greater client trust.

Technology, automation, and artificial intelligence are operating
capabilities. The objective is unchanged:

> Build reliable digital systems that create measurable value for clients
> while continuously increasing the company's profitability, leverage,
> reliability, and scalability.
