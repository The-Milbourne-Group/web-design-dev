## 1. Purpose

Define who to prioritize, which problems indicate strong fit, and which
opportunities carry disproportionate risk.

## 2.1 Size Bands

Headcount is **not** the only qualification variable — revenue, complexity,
urgency and digital maturity carry equal weight (D-021).

| Band | Priority |
|---|---|
| 10–100 employees | **Primary target** |
| 100–250 employees | Secondary, where decision-maker access remains practical |
| 5–10 employees | Selective, where revenue and project economics justify it |
| 250+ employees | Opportunistic until enterprise delivery capacity exists |

Revenue qualification is used alongside budget availability, cost of the
existing problem, strategic importance, decision-maker access and project
scope — never as a rigid threshold on its own.

## 2.2 Not Targeted

Explicitly outside the profile (D-020): pre-revenue startups seeking
inexpensive experimentation; businesses without validated demand; buyers
seeking only the lowest-cost website; organizations that cannot connect a
digital investment to a meaningful business objective.

## 3. ICP Principle

An ideal client is defined by **problem fit and economic fit**, not
demographics alone.

Evaluate: problem severity, digital maturity, willingness to change, ability
to invest, decision-making access, strategic fit, delivery fit, and expansion
potential.

## 4. High-Fit Characteristics

Prefer clients that:

- Have a meaningful business objective
- Have proven they can sell and operate successfully
- Depend materially on digital presence or workflows
- Can identify an accountable decision-maker
- Have an identifiable, articulable problem
- Value quality and reliability over lowest cost
- Can collaborate on discovery and approvals
- Hold realistic expectations
- Show expansion potential into the D-005 progression

## 5. Problem Signals

Strong-fit signals, aligned to the confirmed primary problem:

- Website fails to establish credibility or communicate value
- Low conversion despite adequate traffic
- Outdated digital presence relative to the business's actual standing
- Fragmented customer or lead journey
- Manual operational bottlenecks
- Poor integration between systems
- Technology accumulated without coherent architecture
- Digital infrastructure that has not kept pace with growth
- Need for a scalable digital foundation

The last two are the clearest expression of the confirmed positioning: a
business that has outgrown its digital systems.

## 6. Disqualification Signals

Treat with caution, and be willing to decline:

- Scope is intentionally vague
- Budget and expectations are incompatible
- No accountable decision-maker exists
- The client expects unlimited work
- Legal or security obligations exceed current capability
- Requirements change without governance
- The requested outcome cannot be responsibly delivered
- The client is shopping on price alone — off-strategy per D-007

Declining a poor-fit opportunity is a correct outcome, not a lost sale.

## 7. Buyer Roles

Confirmed under D-021. These are founder-approved and must not be treated as
open — but a role observed on a real opportunity is still recorded as evidence,
because the committee shape varies by size.

**Primary economic buyers.** Founder / Owner · CEO / President · Managing
Director · COO · Managing Partner.

Most appropriate where the problem affects business growth, operational
efficiency, or overall digital infrastructure.

**Secondary buyers and champions.** CMO / Marketing Director · VP of Marketing
· Head of Marketing · Director of Operations · Head of Operations · CTO · IT
Director · Digital Director · E-commerce leadership.

**Typical buying committee.**

| Organization | Committee |
|---|---|
| Smaller | One economic buyer (usually owner or CEO) plus one operational or marketing stakeholder |
| Mid-sized | Economic buyer, departmental champion, technical reviewer where integrations or applications are involved, finance or procurement where required |

**Qualification requirement.** A qualified opportunity should have:

1. A meaningful business problem.
2. A credible financial or operational reason to solve it.
3. Access to an economic decision-maker.
4. A realistic implementation timeline.
5. Sufficient budget or budget potential.
6. A problem that fits the company's capabilities.

**Continue recording actual buyer patterns** in the Buyer Evidence table in
`clients/<client>/README.md` — who initiated, who held budget, who could say no,
who had to be convinced, plus industry and size. D-021 sets the expected shape;
real engagements test whether it holds, and a variance is a change proposal
under `governance/CHANGE_MANAGEMENT.md`.

## 8. Qualification Questions

Qualification must establish:

- What outcome matters?
- What problem exists now?
- What has already been attempted?
- What happens if nothing changes?
- Who owns the decision?
- What constraints apply?
- What timeline matters?
- How will success be evaluated?

Full procedure: `sops/sales/QUALIFICATION.md`.

## 9. Initial Market

Confirmed under D-036: **established small-to-mid-sized service and
professional businesses with growth ambitions whose digital presence or
infrastructure has become fragmented, outdated, underperforming, or
disconnected from their operational needs.**

Prioritise the overlap of:

- digital infrastructure problems;
- a need for strategic website improvement;
- potential expansion into systems or workflow improvement;
- sufficient project economics ($5,000 minimum engagement, `SERVICES.md` §2.4);
- comfort working remotely with a boutique specialist.

The company begins **broad enough to test demand** rather than claiming an
industry specialization before evidence exists (D-019). Specialization follows
the evidence criteria in D-019, evaluated against the market-selection criteria
in D-031.
