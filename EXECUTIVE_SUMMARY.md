The Milbourne Group has adopted a founder-approved working strategic configuration centered on becoming a **boutique strategic digital systems partner**.

The company will focus externally on a single broad business problem: **fragmented, outgrown, or underperforming digital infrastructure**. Internally, it retains broad capabilities across websites, web applications, digital systems, automation, and optimization.

The initial commercial model is intentionally structured as a progression:

**Strategic Website & Digital Foundation → Digital Systems & Workflow Improvement → Optimization, Support & Managed Digital Systems**

The primary client category is established small-to-mid-sized businesses with growth ambitions and the capacity to invest in meaningful digital improvement. The company is positioned as a boutique strategic partner with premium-specialist characteristics, competing on strategic thinking, business understanding, implementation quality, systems expertise, and measurable outcomes rather than low pricing or AI novelty.

Delivery and acquisition are configured as national and remote-first, while local and regional relationships may be leveraged for early trust, referrals, and partnerships.

The operating model is founder-led for strategy, sales, architecture, client relationships, high-consequence decisions, and quality control. Repetitive work should progressively be systemized, automated, or supported by flexible specialist capacity.

## Remaining Dependencies

The system is not yet fully commercially configured. Before finalizing
pricing, packages, acquisition systems, and capacity, the company must resolve
industry focus, detailed ICP and buyer profile, primary buying trigger,
founder advantages, revenue urgency and capacity, acquisition channels, and
pricing economics.

All of these are tracked in **`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`** (Q-001 – Q-008) with
dependencies and blocked work identified. The founder's own analysis
identifies **founder advantages (Q-005) and revenue urgency/capacity (Q-006)**
as the highest-leverage inputs, because they may materially change market
selection — so they should be answered before industry focus is finalized.

These open items are deliberate dependencies, not gaps to be filled with
generic assumptions.
