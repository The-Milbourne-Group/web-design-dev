## 1. Purpose
Generate awareness and qualified demand while building evidence for The Milbourne Group's positioning.

## 2. Marketing Objective

Attract the right prospects by communicating relevant problems, valuable
outcomes, credible expertise, and a clear next step.

### 2.1 Positioning Constraints

These are binding on every marketing artifact (D-001 – D-004, D-007, D-009):

- **Lead with the client problem**, not with capability or technology.
  The confirmed problem is fragmented, outgrown, or underperforming digital
  infrastructure.
- **Never lead with AI.** AI and automation are operating advantages, not the
  value proposition. AI-led messaging places the company in the commodity AI
  category it has decided not to compete in (`RISKS.md` R-002).
- **Never compete on price.** The position is boutique / premium specialist.
- **Present the narrow problem, not the full capability menu.** Breadth is
  internal (D-004); `SERVICES.md` §3 is not a marketing menu.
- **National reach is not indiscriminate marketing.** The geographic model is
  national and remote-first (D-008), but initial acquisition stays focused and
  evidence-driven.
- **No invented facts.** No unearned metrics, client names, case-study
  results, guarantees, or prices. See `BRAND.md` §6 and `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

## 3. Core Channels

Confirmed under D-026: a **concentrated** multi-channel strategy, in priority
order. Concentrate resources on the first three; do not attempt simultaneous
optimization of every channel.

| # | Channel | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warm network and referrals** — former clients, existing professional relationships, business contacts, personal network where professionally appropriate, referral partners | Trust already exists and acquisition cost is lower |
| 2 | **Targeted outbound** — focused campaigns toward businesses showing clear digital maturity gaps | Personalized and problem-led, never generic |
| 3 | **Strategic partnerships** — referral relationships with complementary providers | Partners already hold trusted client relationships |
| 4 | Authority and portfolio content | Evidence of expertise, not content volume |
| 5 | Inbound search | A longer-term asset, not the immediate revenue source |

### 3.1 Outbound Target Signals

Observable indicators of a digital maturity gap (D-026): outdated websites;
poor mobile experience; broken conversion paths; business expansion; new
locations; recent hiring; rebranding activity; growing teams; complex manual
workflows; digital systems inconsistent with company maturity.

### 3.2 Referral Partner Categories

Partners who meet digital problems exceeding their own delivery capability
(D-023): marketing, branding and advertising agencies; graphic designers;
business consultants; managed IT providers; software consultants; commercial
photographers; SEO specialists; professional service firms serving established
businesses.

**Channel 1 depends on Q-013.** The warm network cannot be worked until the
founder's actual relationships and former clients are documented.

## 4. Content Strategy
Create content around:
- Client problems
- Decision frameworks
- Common digital mistakes
- Before/after improvements
- Process transparency
- Technical and strategic insights
- Evidence from completed work

Content must not expose confidential client information.

## 5. Website Role
The company website should:
- Explain positioning
- Clarify offers
- Build trust
- Demonstrate capability
- Capture qualified demand
- Provide an appropriate CTA

## 6. SEO
Prioritize useful, intent-aligned content and technically sound implementation. Avoid manipulative or low-value content production.

## 7. Marketing Metrics
Measure:
- Qualified traffic
- Qualified leads
- Conversion rate
- Channel contribution
- Cost where measurable
- Pipeline influence

Detailed definitions belong in `METRICS.md`.

## 8. Experimentation
Document hypothesis, change, expected outcome, measurement method, and result. Do not confuse increased activity with improved marketing.
