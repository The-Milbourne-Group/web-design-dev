## 1. Purpose

This document governs business model, strategic positioning, market
selection, offer architecture, pricing principles, revenue quality, growth
priorities, and business constraints.

It does not replace the universal operating rules in `MASTER.md`, and it does
not override founder-approved decisions in `STRATEGIC_CONFIGURATION.md`.

## 2. Strategic Identity

The Milbourne Group is a **boutique strategic digital systems partner**
helping established businesses strengthen the digital infrastructure that
supports credibility, conversion, operational efficiency, and growth.

The company addresses one primary problem externally — **fragmented,
outgrown, or underperforming digital infrastructure** — while retaining broad
internal capability across websites, web applications, digital systems, and
automation.

Technology, automation, and AI are implementation and leverage capabilities.
The commercial value is the outcome created for the client. The company does
not position as an AI-first business or compete as a generic AI agency.

*Authority: `DECISIONS.md` D-001, D-002, D-003, D-004. This section previously
described an "AI-first digital services company," contradicting
founder-approved positioning; corrected 2026-08-29.*

## 3. Mission

Help established businesses improve how they present themselves, attract
demand, convert opportunities, and operate — through better digital systems.

## 4. Long-Term Vision

A highly systemized digital services company with increasing reliability,
efficiency, profitability, and capacity — built on documented systems,
specialized capabilities, automation, reusable assets, and appropriate human
oversight.

## 5. Business Model

Revenue may come from one-time projects, recurring retainers, maintenance,
optimization, managed digital systems, automation implementation, and
strategic advisory.

Prefer revenue that is profitable, collectible, repeatable, strategically
aligned, and deliverable without disproportionate custom complexity.

## 6. Value Creation Model

`Business Problem → Research → Strategy → System Design → Implementation → Verification → Deployment → Measurement → Optimization`

Do not sell implementation without sufficient understanding of the problem.

## 7. Strategic Position

Differentiate through business understanding, strategic reasoning, design
quality, reliable development, intelligent automation, appropriate human
oversight, and measurable outcomes.

The company is **not** positioned as a commodity website provider, generic AI
agency, lowest-cost provider, or high-volume standardized vendor. Competing on
price is off-strategy.

Do not rely on "we use AI" as differentiation. AI-led messaging invites
comparison with the exact category the company has decided not to compete in
(`RISKS.md` R-002).

## 8. Market Selection

Evaluate markets on problem severity, willingness to pay, accessibility,
delivery fit, profitability, repeatability, competitive conditions, and
referral/expansion potential.

Do not assume "any business that needs a website" is a durable market
strategy.

**Confirmed:** primary client category is established small-to-mid-sized
businesses with growth ambitions and capacity to invest (D-006). Geographic
model is national and remote-first, selectively leveraging local and regional
relationships (D-008). National capability does not license indiscriminate
marketing.

**Open:** industry focus, detailed size bands, and buyer roles — `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`
D-019, D-020 and D-021. Detailed audience definitions belong in `ICP.md`.

## 9. Offer Architecture

The confirmed commercial progression (D-005):

| Stage | Offer | Commercial role |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | Strategic Website & Digital Foundation | Primary way clients begin |
| **Expansion** | Digital Systems & Workflow Improvement | Deepen after proven value |
| **Recurring** | Optimization, Support & Managed Digital Systems | Durable revenue |

Audits, assessments, and strategy sessions remain valid **sales instruments**
that establish understanding without creating unlimited unpaid consulting.
They are not the confirmed entry offer, and must not be sold as a substitute
for it.

*This supersedes the earlier three-tier "entry / core / expansion" model in
which entry offers were defined as audits and assessments — see D-005.*

Detailed service definitions belong in `SERVICES.md`.

## 10. Pricing Principles

Price according to value, complexity, risk, expertise, capacity, and expected
client impact. A pricing model must protect margin, define scope, account for
risk and revisions, and remain understandable.

**Specific pricing must not be invented.** Pricing, minimum engagement, and
package economics are set in `SERVICES.md` §2.4 (D-038). Until the founder
configures them, every reference to price is "to be determined." This applies
to proposals, the company website, and all agent output without exception.

## 11. Revenue Quality

Evaluate revenue on amount, expected gross contribution, delivery risk, scope
control, support burden, strategic alignment, reusability, and expansion
potential.

Revenue without acceptable margin is not automatically good business.
